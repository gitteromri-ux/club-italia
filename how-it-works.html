01

The Shape

Twelve weeks, arranged like a novel.

A Club Italia course is not a stream of lessons. It is a season with an opening, a middle, and a finish. Every course covers roughly one CEFR half-level, and every course is designed so that by the last class you can do something specific and useful with the language, not simply know more of it.

Below is the practical week, the practical season, and the practical rhythm. If you are used to an app that expects you to remember to open it, none of this will feel familiar. It will feel like a school again.

04

Inside the Classroom

A live class is a room, not a screen.

Our classroom is not a Zoom call. It is a broadcast studio built for language teaching. Full-screen native materials, a live whiteboard, side-by-side pair rooms, and a teacher who can call on you by name without hunting for the button.

Every class you attend is recorded and saved to your library for life. Attendance is expected but not policed. Miss a Tuesday and watch it back on Friday morning, before the next class picks up the thread.

05

The 85 Minutes

The four beats of one live class.

i

Cultural scenario

Every class opens on a real Italian moment. You speak inside a scene before you know the rule that makes the scene work.

ii

Live interaction

Pairs, threes, full room. Every student speaks every class. The teacher directs, never lectures.

iii

Guided structure

Grammar arrives, once, after the sentences. Named calmly. Illustrated with what you already said.

iv

Real task

Everyone leaves with a small useful mission. A text to send, a question to ask, a scene to rehearse.

06

The Certificate

A serious credential, quietly earned.

The Club Italia certificate is CEFR-aligned, meaning it maps to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. It records your level in a way any Italian institution, university, or immigration officer will recognise on sight.

To earn it you complete the twenty live classes, a written portfolio, and a short oral exam with a second Club Italia teacher. It is a real assessment, not a certificate of attendance.
