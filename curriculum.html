Curriculum · CEFR A1 to C2

A curriculum written against the framework.

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages describes six levels by what a learner can do. Every Club Italia unit is written to move you one step along those descriptors, with stated objectives, grammar, vocabulary and a cultural setting.

Six levels

The syllabus, level by level.

Open a level to see its can-do outcomes, grammar focus and cultural themes.

A1

Breakthrough

Basic user · CI Principiante, CI Elementare, Al Caffè
+

You can

  • Introduce yourself and others
  • Order food and drink, pay, ask prices
  • Ask for and follow simple directions
  • Fill in a form, write a short message

Grammar focus

  • Sound system and spelling
  • Articles, gender and number
  • Present tense: regular, essere, avere, fare
  • Possessives, c'è / ci sono, piacere

Culture and themes

  • Greetings and the bar ritual
  • Family and the Italian home
  • Rome: piazze, fontane, quartieri
A2

Waystage

Basic user · CI Intermedio, CI Avanzato, A Tavola, In Viaggio
+

You can

  • Describe past experiences and routines
  • Handle travel, hotels, trains, shops
  • Make arrangements and invitations
  • Write a simple personal letter

Grammar focus

  • Passato prossimo and imperfetto
  • Reflexive verbs, direct and indirect pronouns
  • Futuro semplice, comparatives
  • Polite imperative, ci and ne

Culture and themes

  • Florence and the Tuscan table
  • Bologna: markets and portici
  • Regional food and festivals
B1

Threshold

Independent user · Chiacchierando
+

You can

  • Hold a conversation on familiar topics
  • Give and justify opinions
  • Narrate a story, a film, a book
  • Write a structured letter or short essay

Grammar focus

  • Condizionale presente
  • Introduction to the congiuntivo presente
  • Combined pronouns, relative pronouns
  • Trapassato prossimo, connectors

Culture and themes

  • Italian cinema and song
  • Work and daily life in Milan
  • The language of citizenship and bureaucracy
B2

Vantage

Independent user · Advanced cohort
+

You can

  • Argue a position and weigh alternatives
  • Follow news, podcasts and talk shows
  • Read contemporary fiction and journalism
  • Write a reasoned argumentative text

Grammar focus

  • Congiuntivo: all tenses and concordance
  • Periodo ipotetico, all three types
  • Passive and si passivante
  • Gerund, participle, infinitive clauses

Culture and themes

  • Contemporary Italian literature
  • Italian press and politics
  • Design, fashion and industry
C1

Effective operational proficiency

Proficient user · Advanced cohort
+

You can

  • Express yourself fluently and spontaneously
  • Use Italian for professional and academic purposes
  • Produce clear, detailed, well-structured texts
  • Recognise implicit meaning and register

Grammar focus

  • Passato remoto in narrative
  • Stylistic word order, discourse markers
  • Formal and bureaucratic register
  • Idiom, proverbs, collocation

Culture and themes

  • Dante, Petrarca, Boccaccio in context
  • Art history in Italian
  • Opera libretti
C2

Mastery

Proficient user · Advanced cohort
+

You can

  • Understand virtually everything heard or read
  • Summarise and reconstruct arguments from multiple sources
  • Express fine shades of meaning precisely
  • Work with literary and specialised texts

Grammar focus

  • Full command of literary tenses
  • Regional varieties and dialect awareness
  • Rhetoric and translation
  • Academic and legal Italian

Culture and themes

  • Literary seminar: Calvino, Morante, Eco
  • Regional Italy and its dialects
  • Translation workshop

B2 to C2 are taught in advanced cohorts that follow the same framework. Hours per CEFR level vary by learner; the Council of Europe gives no fixed number.

Sample syllabus · CI Principiante

Twenty lessons, one city.

Your first Italian, taught in Rome, the city where the language began. In twenty live lessons you go from the sound of the language to your first real conversations.

LessonUnitContent and setting
01«Ciao, Roma!»The Italian sound system, greetings, subject pronouns, the verb essere. Set at Fiumicino, first steps in Rome.
02«Chi sei?»Introducing yourself, nationalities, avere, definite articles. A dialogue at a Roman café.
03«La mia famiglia»Family vocabulary, possessive adjectives, plural nouns. Photos from a real Roman family.
04«Al bar»Ordering coffee, present tense of regular verbs (-are), c'è / ci sono. The espresso ritual.
05«In città»Prepositions of place, andare and venire, asking for directions. The seven hills of Rome.
06«A che ora?»Telling time, days of the week, months, the verb fare. A day in the life of a Roman.
07«A tavola»Food vocabulary, partitives, quanto costa, ordering in a trattoria. Cucina romana.
08«Mi piace»Piacere, opinions, comparatives, likes and dislikes. Italian film and music.
09«Che tempo fa?»Weather, seasons, dovere / potere / volere. Weekends in the Roman countryside.
10«A casa»Rooms of the house, furniture, dare and stare. Roman apartment life.
11«Vestiti»Clothes, colours, adjectives that agree, sapere vs conoscere. Via del Corso, the shopping street.
12«Al mercato»Food shopping, markets, imperative for polite requests. Campo de' Fiori.
13«In ufficio»Work vocabulary, jobs, telefonare, scrivere. A day in a Roman office.
14«In viaggio»Trains, tickets, hotels, the near future with andare a + infinitive. Termini Station.
15«A che punto sei?»Review, mini test, oral presentation. Half-way milestone.
16«Passato prossimo»Introduction to the past tense with avere. Yesterday in Rome.
17«Passato prossimo con essere»The past with essere, agreement of the past participle. Stories from a Sunday.
18«Il mio quartiere»Describing places, reflexive verbs, daily routine. Trastevere on a Saturday.
19«Il fine settimana»Weekend plans, imperfetto introduced, contrastive with passato. A country outing.
20«Arrivederci, Roma!»Full oral CEFR A1.1 assessment, farewell dialogue, next steps. Ready for CI Elementare.
