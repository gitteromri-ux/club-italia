01

The Premise

A method that respects your years.

You did not come to a language class to memorise flashcards. You came to hold a real conversation over an autumn dinner in Bologna, to write your cousin a birthday note in her mother's tongue, to follow an Italian film without subtitles. That is a serious ambition. It deserves a serious method.

The Club Italia method is not the fastest and not the shortest. It is the method most likely to still be with you two years from now, when the season is over and you find yourself, quite naturally, thinking in Italian.

A language is not a set of rules. It is a habit of attention.

Marco Bianchi · Head of Method, Bologna

02

Four Teaching Moves

Every class turns on four hinges.

There is nothing improvised about a Club Italia class. Every 85-minute session moves through the same four beats, in the same order, so students always know where they are.

i

Cultural scenario

Every class opens with a real Italian moment. The market in Testaccio. A phone call to the doctor. A Sunday lunch in Puglia. Grammar begins as a way to speak inside a scene, never as an abstract rule on a slide.

Roughly minutes 1 to 15
ii

Live interaction

Everyone speaks. In pairs, in threes, in the full room. The teacher directs the traffic but the sentences come from you. This is the beat that makes ten adults into a class instead of a webinar.

Roughly minutes 15 to 45
iii

Guided structure

Only now does the teacher name what you were just doing. The subjunctive. The pronoun ci. The past conditional. You meet the rule after you have already met the sentence, which is why it stays.

Roughly minutes 45 to 70
iv

Real task

Every class closes with something to carry into the week. Write your cousin a text. Order the taxi. Argue with a friend about the film you both watched. Language is what you do with it, not what you know about it.

Roughly minutes 70 to 85

The rule arrives after the sentence, never before.

The second law of Club Italia

04

The Method in Action

A single lesson, frame by frame.

This is the actual storyboard of lesson seven, Elementare, Week Four. A dinner invitation in Bologna. Watch how four moves and six pillars produce one very Italian evening.

01

Il market, Testaccio

Class opens on a market scene. Vegetables, prices, the exchange. Students already speak inside the moment.

02

In pairs, the invito

Ten adults break into five pairs. Every pair invites the other to dinner. Two minutes each. The teacher walks the room.

03

The congiuntivo, quietly

Now the teacher names what everyone was already doing. Spero che tu venga. Because they used it, the rule sticks.

04

Homework, a real text

Write a text message inviting a friend to Sunday lunch. Send it in Italian. The teacher reads all ten before next class.

05

Why Small

Ten to twelve. Never fewer. Never more.

A class of two is a lesson. A class of forty is a broadcast. A class of ten to twelve is a conversation, and that is what a language actually is. In this size we can guarantee three things at once: every student speaks every class, every voice is heard by the teacher every week, and there are enough different accents in the room to make the practice real.

This is why we hold the number sacred. When a class fills, we open another. We never overbook. We never upsell you a private version of the wrong solution.

06

The Teacher

Native, only. Trained, always.

Every Club Italia teacher was born in Italy, holds a certification in Italian as a foreign language, and teaches from an Italian city. Not a suburb of one. Not a satellite. From the room you would find them in on a Tuesday morning.
