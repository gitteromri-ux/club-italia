The museum is the classroom

Why l'arte lives in Italian

The Italian language of art was invented inside these galleries. We teach it there.

The Italian Renaissance was, in the end, a linguistic project. The painters spoke Tuscan, the patrons spoke Tuscan, the manuals spoke Tuscan. To read Vasari's Vite in Italian is to feel the paintings breathe in a way no English translation quite captures.

This six-session capsule teaches the specific vocabulary of Italian art history — the light, the composition, the pigment, the patron. It is set inside real Italian museums and churches, from the Uffizi to the Santa Maria del Popolo Caravaggios. Alessandro hosts every session.

You will finish able to read an Italian museum wall label, follow an Italian art documentary, and hold a coffee-shop conversation about a Botticelli without ever switching to English.

The full curriculum

Six live lessons, one journey

A structured Cultural · Any level curriculum, each 85-minute live class themed to the setting of the course.

01

Il vocabolario del dipinto

The Italian vocabulary of painting — tela, tavola, affresco, tempera, olio, sfumato, chiaroscuro.

02

Giotto e il Trecento

Giotto and the birth of Western perspective. The vocabulary of pre-Renaissance devotion.

03

Il Quattrocento — Firenze

Brunelleschi, Donatello, Botticelli. Reading Vasari on the Uffizi masters.

04

Il Cinquecento — Roma

Michelangelo, Raphael, Bramante. The Sistine Chapel and Saint Peter's in their own vocabulary.

05

Caravaggio e il Barocco

The Caravaggio revolution — luce, ombra, realismo. Reading a Bellori page.

06

Bernini e la Roma barocca

Bernini's Rome — Piazza Navona, Piazza San Pietro, the language of Baroque sculpture. Cultural graduation.

Listen in

Inside the Cerasi Chapel

A short excerpt from a real lesson. Press listen to hear the pronunciation.

Session 05 · Il Caravaggio a Santa Maria del Popolo

Inside the Cerasi Chapel, forty centimetres from the Conversione di San Paolo. Alessandro speaks quietly, because the church has other visitors.

Alessandro
Guarda la luce. Da dove arriva?
Tu
Da sinistra, molto forte.
Alessandro
Esatto. E il cavallo, dove guarda?
Tu
Verso il basso, verso San Paolo.
Alessandro
Bene. È la composizione che ha cambiato la pittura per due secoli.

Teacher's note. Five turns inside a chapel. This is the exact language you will use the next time you stand in an Italian church and want to think in Italian about the painting in front of you.
