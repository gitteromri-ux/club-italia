What you will learn.

Six real behaviours aligned to CEFR A1.2 → A2.1. Every outcome is measured in your final oral and written assessment.

  • ✓Move between passato prossimo, imperfetto and trapassato with ease
  • ✓Express opinion, doubt and preference clearly
  • ✓Talk about food, cooking, cuisine and regional identity
  • ✓Understand fast native speech in most everyday situations
  • ✓Read blogs, restaurant reviews and short essays
  • ✓Take part in a small group conversation for the full 85 minutes

The syllabus, week by week

Your twenty lessons.

#TopicGrammar & cultural setting
01Bologna la dottaItaly's oldest university, the arcades, the food. Review of A1.2.
02Passato remoto in the wildRecognising passato remoto in fairy tales and news. Passive knowledge only.
03Trapassato prossimoTalking about what happened before what happened. Storytelling structure.
04Imperfetto vs passato prossimoThe Italian storytelling engine. Real narratives from a Bolognese childhood.
05La cucina emilianaRagù, tortellini, mortadella, Parmigiano. Cooking verbs and cultural vocabulary.
06A tavola con gli amiciDinner-party Italian. Interrupting politely, agreeing, disagreeing, refilling wine.
07Ho un'opinioneExpressing opinion: penso che, credo che, secondo me. Congiuntivo introduced softly.
08Il mio quartiereComparing places: più / meno / così… come. Bologna vs Milano vs Roma.
09Andiamo al cinemaReserving tickets, film genres, describing a plot, recommending.
10La musica italianaLucio Dalla, Battisti, De Andrè, contemporary indie. Listening lab.
11A metà del camminoMidpoint oral assessment, cultural presentation.
12Se dovessi scegliereHypothetical in the present: se + congiuntivo imperfetto + condizionale.
13Le città italianeDescribing Italian cities, geography, north / centre / south differences.
14Il lavoro in ItaliaVocabulary of work, CV, colloquio di lavoro, contratti.
15La sanitàHealthcare vocabulary, at the doctor, at the hospital, the Italian public system.
16La bancaBank vocabulary, opening an account, paying bills, ordering online.
17La casa in affittoRenting an apartment, understanding a contract, negotiating with a landlord.
18Feste italianeItalian holidays: Natale, Pasqua, Ferragosto, Palio di Siena, Carnevale di Venezia.
19Il dialettoIntroduction to Italian dialects — bolognese, romanesco, napoletano, siciliano — as cultural heritage.
20Arrivederci, Bologna!Full CEFR A2.1 assessment, oral presentation, path to CI Avanzato.

Common questions

Answer your top questions.

Is this really live?＋
Every session is a live class taught by a native teacher in Italy, at the scheduled hour, in a group of ten to twelve. If you cannot attend live, the class is recorded for you to review the same evening.
What if I have never spoken Italian before?＋
If you are placed in this course by your advisor, it is the right starting point for your level. If you are not sure, book a free placement call and we will listen to your level in ten minutes.
How long is one lesson?＋
Every live lesson is eighty-five minutes. Long enough to enter a real conversation, short enough to remain fully present.
Can I pick my teacher?＋
On annual plans yes — you can request Giulia Ferri by name. On monthly and term plans we place you with the best available fit for your level and time zone.
What device do I need?＋
A laptop or desktop with a working camera and a good microphone. Headphones are recommended. Everything runs inside your browser.
What if I miss a class?＋
Every class is recorded. Giulia will send you a fifteen-minute personal note by email so you never fall behind.

Reserve your placement call.

Thirty minutes with an academic advisor. Spoken in English, honest about your level, ending with a clear next step.

We reply within one working day. No sales script, no pressure.
