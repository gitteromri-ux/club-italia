Inside a single lesson

Eighty-five minutes with Giulia.

Lesson 07 · Ho un'opinione. Every learner logs in at 8pm EST on a Tuesday. The camera opens on Giulia's desk in Bologna. This is what the next eighty-five minutes look like.

Learning goals

  • You can hold the scene of the lesson in Italian for four full turns.
  • You can use the new grammar without translating in your head.
  • You can pronounce the new vocabulary the way Giulia does.
  • You leave with two written examples in your own notebook.

The 85-minute flow

  1. 00:00 to 00:10 · Warm greetings and last week's carryover.
  2. 00:10 to 00:30 · The new scene, first read together, then acted out in pairs.
  3. 00:30 to 00:50 · Grammar in the moment. Giulia draws on the whiteboard.
  4. 00:50 to 01:10 · Pair rooms with focused correction from Giulia.
  5. 01:10 to 01:25 · Group reunion, final rehearsal, homework for next week.

Between-lesson work

  • A twelve-minute Biagio conversation on the new scene.
  • One short reading, one short listening, one short writing.
  • Optional: a matching cultural clip from our library.
  • Expected weekly load: about ninety minutes outside class.

The city is the classroom

Bologna, la Dotta, la Grassa, la Rossa.

The learned, the fat, the red. Bologna is the oldest university city in the western world, the culinary capital of Italy, and a city of terracotta rooftops and long portici. Every one of those three names matters for how Italian is spoken here.

Giulia teaches from a kitchen apartment near Piazza Maggiore, and lessons often run over a working stove. She is a trained chef as well as a certified teacher, and she knows why the Bolognese roll their r and lengthen their consonants.

By the end of the term you will speak the Italian of the north-central heartland, the Italian a lawyer or a doctor from Emilia would use with a friend at Sunday lunch.

Bologna, Italy

Giulia Ferri

Bologna DITALS II · trained chef

Giulia has taught Italian to adult learners for over a decade. She holds a full degree in Italian language and literature and a recognised international teaching certification.

Her lessons are built on scenes, not slides. She believes an adult learner deserves the same respect a graduate student receives in a Perugia or Siena classroom, and she plans every ninety minutes on that principle.

She teaches only from Bologna. When you take her class, you take it into the country she loves and knows.
