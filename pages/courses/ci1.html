Three things separate this course from every A0 Italian class you have tried before.

La città come aula

The city is the classroom

Why Roma teaches the first word

A0 belongs to Rome for the same reason the Republic did: nowhere else forgives beginnings as generously.

Rome is where the Italian language was born, argued about, put on trial, and finally set free. We start here because every other Italian city is, in some way, in conversation with Rome — its Latin roots, its Renaissance humanists, its trattorie shouting orders across a piazza at seven in the evening.

A0 belongs to Rome because Rome forgives. Romans are patient in a way northerners often are not. They will slow down, they will repeat, they will laugh with you, not at you. If you are going to make your first mistakes in Italian, make them under a Roman sky, in a Roman café, with a Roman waiter who has heard every mispronunciation of espresso ever attempted.

Every lesson is threaded through the city: the market at Campo de' Fiori, the trams of Trastevere, the tram terminus at Piazza Venezia, a Sunday lunch in a family kitchen in Testaccio. You are not learning tourist Italian. You are learning to belong.

The full curriculum

Twenty live lessons, one journey

A structured A0 → A1.1 curriculum, each 85-minute live class themed to the setting of the course.

01

Ciao, Roma!

The Italian sound system, greetings, subject pronouns, the verb essere. Set at Fiumicino, first steps in Rome.

02

Chi sei?

Introducing yourself, nationalities, avere, definite articles. A dialogue at a Roman café.

03

La mia famiglia

Family vocabulary, possessive adjectives, plural nouns. Photos from a real Roman family.

04

Al bar

Ordering coffee, present tense of regular verbs (-are), c'è / ci sono. The espresso ritual.

05

In città

Prepositions of place, andare and venire, asking for directions. The seven hills of Rome.

06

A che ora?

Telling time, days of the week, months, the verb fare. A day in the life of a Roman.

07

A tavola

Food vocabulary, partitives, quanto costa, ordering in a trattoria. Cucina romana.

08

Mi piace

Piacere, opinions, comparatives, likes and dislikes. Italian film and music.

09

Che tempo fa?

Weather, seasons, dovere / potere / volere. Weekends in the Roman countryside.

10

A casa

Rooms of the house, furniture, dare and stare. Roman apartment life.

11

Vestiti

Clothes, colours, adjectives that agree, sapere vs conoscere. Via del Corso, the shopping street.

12

Al mercato

Food shopping, markets, imperative for polite requests. Campo de' Fiori.

13

In ufficio

Work vocabulary, jobs, telefonare, scrivere. A day in a Roman office.

14

In viaggio

Trains, tickets, hotels, the near future with andare a + infinitive. Termini Station.

15

A che punto sei?

Review, mini test, oral presentation. Half-way milestone.

16

Passato prossimo

Introduction to the past tense with avere. Yesterday in Rome.

17

Passato prossimo con essere

The past with essere, agreement of the past participle. Stories from a Sunday.

18

Il mio quartiere

Describing places, reflexive verbs, daily routine. Trastevere on a Saturday.

19

Il fine settimana

Weekend plans, imperfetto introduced, contrastive with passato. A country outing.

20

Arrivederci, Roma!

Full oral CEFR A1.1 assessment, farewell dialogue, next steps. Ready for CI Elementare.

Listen in

A morning at Sant'Eustachio

A short excerpt from a real lesson. Press listen to hear the pronunciation.

Lesson 02 · A un caffè romano

A tavolo at Sant'Eustachio, seven forty-five in the morning. The barista, Marco, has seen you three days in a row.

Marco
Buongiorno! Il solito?
Tu
Buongiorno Marco. Sì, un cappuccino e un cornetto, per favore.
Marco
Alla crema o al cioccolato?
Tu
Al cioccolato. Grazie.
Marco
Ecco a te. Uno e ottanta.

Teacher's note. Notice the shift from 'Lei' at first contact to 'tu' by day three. Rome moves through formality faster than the textbooks admit.
