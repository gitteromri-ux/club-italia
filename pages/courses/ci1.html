The structure of this course

Where the language begins: sound, words, first exchanges

CI Principiante takes a true beginner from A0 to A1.1. At A1.1, the CEFR describes a learner who understands and uses familiar everyday expressions, introduces themselves, and interacts simply when the other person speaks slowly.

01

Hear it first

Lesson one: the difference between «nono» and «nonno». You will hear it by the end of the hour.

02

Rome as the classroom

03

Live small groups

04

Documented to A1.1

Learning outcomes

By unit 20, you will be able to

Can-do statements aligned with the CEFR descriptors for A1.1.

The full syllabus

Twenty units, one city

A structured A0 to A1.1 syllabus. Each 85 minute live unit pairs one communicative goal with the grammar and vocabulary it needs.

01

Ciao, Roma!

The Italian sound system, greetings, subject pronouns, the verb essere. Set at Fiumicino, first steps in Rome.

02

Chi sei?

Introducing yourself, nationalities, avere, definite articles. A dialogue at a Roman bar.

03

La mia famiglia

Family vocabulary, possessive adjectives, plural nouns. Photos from a real Roman family.

04

Al bar

Ordering coffee, present tense of regular verbs (-are), c'è / ci sono. The espresso ritual.

05

In città

Prepositions of place, andare and venire, asking for directions. The seven hills of Rome.

06

A che ora?

Telling time, days of the week, months, the verb fare. A day in the life of a Roman.

07

A tavola

Food vocabulary, partitives, quanto costa, ordering in a trattoria. Cucina romana.

08

Mi piace

Piacere, opinions, comparatives, likes and dislikes. Italian film and music.

09

Che tempo fa?

Weather, seasons, dovere / potere / volere. Weekends in the Roman countryside.

10

A casa

Rooms of the house, furniture, dare and stare. Roman apartment life.

11

Vestiti

Clothes, colours, adjectives that agree, sapere vs conoscere. Via del Corso, the shopping street.

12

Al mercato

Food shopping, markets, imperative for polite requests. Campo de' Fiori.

13

In ufficio

Work vocabulary, jobs, telefonare, scrivere. A day in a Roman office.

14

In viaggio

Trains, tickets, hotels, the near future with andare a + infinitive. Termini Station.

15

A che punto sei?

Review, progress check and a short oral presentation. The halfway milestone.

16

Passato prossimo

Introduction to the past tense with avere. Yesterday in Rome.

17

Passato prossimo con essere

The past with essere, agreement of the past participle. Stories from a Sunday.

18

Il mio quartiere

Describing places, reflexive verbs, daily routine. Trastevere on a Saturday.

19

Il fine settimana

Weekend plans. The imperfetto introduced and contrasted with the passato prossimo. A country outing.

20

Arrivederci, Roma!

Final spoken and written A1.1 assessment, farewell dialogue, next steps. Ready for CI Elementare.

DITALS II · Laurea in Linguistica

Marco grew up in the Prati neighbourhood of Rome, five streets from St Peter's, in a family of teachers. He studied linguistics at Sapienza, wrote a thesis on how adults acquire a second phonology, and has taught Italian to adults ever since.

«I love the first class. I love the moment a person who has never spoken Italian says «buongiorno» back to me and hears their own voice make a sound they did not make yesterday.»

«Nonno» is not «nono».

One doubled consonant turns a grandfather into the number nine. Marco corrects it in lesson one, and every lesson after.
