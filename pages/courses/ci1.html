The full curriculum

Twenty live units. One journey.

A structured A0 → A1.1 curriculum. Every h4 in Italian is the real Italian topic of the unit, taught live by Marco from Roma.

01

Salutare: buongiorno, ciao, arrivederci + tu/Lei

Learn the essential Italian greetings and the crucial distinction between the familiar tu and the formal Lei. Practise arriving and leaving with buongiorno, buonasera, ciao, arrivederci, a presto.

02

Presentarsi: mi chiamo, sono di, ho... anni

Introduce yourself in Italian. Use mi chiamo Marco, sono di Chicago, ho quarantadue anni. Ask back with e tu, come ti chiami?

03

Nazionalita, professione: sono italiano/a, faccio il/la...

Say where you are from and what you do. Sono americano, sono italiana, faccio l'avvocato, sono insegnante. Learn masculine and feminine adjective agreement in real sentences.

04

Numeri 0 a 100, eta, telefono

Master the Italian numbers from zero to one hundred. Give your age, your phone number, a price at the shop: ho trenta anni, il mio numero e...

05

Alfabeto italiano + fare lo spelling

Learn the twenty-one letters of the Italian alphabet, the five extra imported letters, and how to spell your name out loud: C come Como, A come Ancona.

06

Verbo essere: presente indicativo

Conjugate the fundamental verb essere across all six persons: io sono, tu sei, lui/lei e, noi siamo, voi siete, loro sono. Use it for identity, origin and description.

07

Verbo avere: presente indicativo

Conjugate avere: ho, hai, ha, abbiamo, avete, hanno. Use it for age, possession and the essential idioms ho fame, ho sete, ho freddo.

08

Articoli determinativi il/lo/la/i/gli/le

The six forms of the Italian definite article. Learn when to use il libro, lo studente, la casa, i libri, gli studenti, le case, with the sound-based rules native speakers use automatically.

09

Articoli indeterminativi un/uno/una

The Italian indefinite article. Un caffe, uno zucchero, una pizza, un'amica. Practise picking the right form in five seconds.

10

Nomi maschili e femminili + plurali regolari

The gender of Italian nouns and how to form regular plurals: ragazzo/ragazzi, ragazza/ragazze, studente/studenti. Learn the endings that always tell you gender.

11

Chiedere e indicare la direzione

Ask where things are and understand the answer. Scusi, dov'e il Colosseo? Sempre dritto, poi a destra. E qui a sinistra.

12

Ordinare al bar: un caffe, per favore

Order like a Roman at the bar counter. Un caffe, un cappuccino, un cornetto, per favore. Quanto costa? Il conto, grazie.

13

Verbi in -are (parlare, abitare, lavorare) presente

Conjugate the first family of Italian verbs. Io parlo italiano, tu abiti a Roma, noi lavoriamo insieme. Learn the pattern that unlocks hundreds of verbs.

14

Aggettivi qualificativi (bello, grande, piccolo) + concordanza

Italian adjectives change to match their noun. Un bel ragazzo, una bella ragazza, i bei quadri, le belle piazze. The rule of four endings that governs almost every adjective.

15

Espressioni di tempo: oggi, domani, la mattina, la sera

Situate yourself in the Italian day and week. Oggi, domani, dopodomani, la mattina, il pomeriggio, la sera, la notte.

16

Che ore sono? + gli orari

Tell the time in Italian. Che ore sono? Sono le nove e mezza. Il ristorante apre alle otto. Chiude a mezzanotte.

17

I giorni della settimana e i mesi

Name the days and months in Italian. Lunedi, martedi, mercoledi... gennaio, febbraio... Oggi e giovedi, siamo a marzo.

18

Descrivere la propria casa: c'e, ci sono

Describe where you live. C'e una cucina, ci sono due camere, il bagno e piccolo, il salotto e grande. Master the essential c'e / ci sono distinction.

19

La famiglia italiana: mio padre, mia madre + possessivi

Introduce your family with Italian possessive adjectives. Mio padre, mia madre, mio fratello, mia sorella, i miei nonni, le mie zie.

20

Milestone: raccontare una giornata a Roma per 2 minuti

A live oral milestone. Speak for two full minutes about a day in Rome, using at least ten grammatical structures from units one to nineteen. Marco gives you a written personal feedback note.

Questions

Everything you might be wondering

Is this really live?＋
Every session is a live class taught by a native teacher in Italy, at the scheduled hour, in a group of ten to twelve. If you cannot attend live, the class is recorded for you to review the same evening.
How many units in the course?＋
20 live units, 90 minutes each. Every unit is filmed live from Italy and is broadcast on the same day, at the same time each week.
What if I have never learned Italian before?＋
If our advisor places you in this course, it is the right starting point for your level. If you are not sure, book a free placement call and we will listen to your Italian for ten minutes and give you an honest answer.
Who is the teacher?＋
Marco Rinaldi, native Italian teacher from Roma. Every unit of this course is taught by Marco personally, from the same city, broadcasting live to your small group of ten to twelve.
Will I get a certificate?＋
Yes. On completion you receive a CEFR-aligned certificate issued by eTeacher confirming the target level, plus lifetime access to every recording and one hundred dollars in learning credits.
What comes next after this course?＋
Your teacher will personally recommend your next step. Most learners continue into the next CEFR level in our ladder; some pause and take a Culture Capsule to consolidate. Nothing is automatic.
