The region

Sicily, as Sofia teaches it

Sofia is Palermitana. She read modern languages at the Università di Palermo, took the CEDILS specialisation in Venice, then taught in Marseille and Tel Aviv before returning to teach online from Palermo. She is bilingual in Italian and French and has passable Arabic. Her advanced students often say she is the most exacting teacher they have ever had, and mean it as a compliment.

Every teacher on our faculty teaches from one Italian region and from one only. That is not a marketing point. It is what allows a class to have a place. When Sofia opens a lesson at seven in the morning her time, the light behind her is Palermo light. The bread on her desk was bought at a Palermo bakery. The vocabulary that emerges in a conversation about weekends is Sicily vocabulary. Students on the other side of the world tell us the same thing, class after class. They are not on a language platform. They are in Palermo, for eighty five minutes.

Regional Italian is not dialect. Sofia teaches the standard national language, the Italian of newspapers and universities and the state broadcaster. What she brings is location. A verb form is illustrated with a scene from her city. A grammar point is drawn out of a conversation that could have taken place on her street. The language stays standard. The teaching room is specific.

Teaching manifesto
I share the top level with Luca. We teach it the same way, from opposite ends of the peninsula, and that gives our advanced students two different accents to listen to before the certificate. That matters. A learner who has only heard one voice teach the language is a fragile learner. My Sicilian students, my French students, my Israeli students, my American students all get corrected the same way in my class, in a language that treats them as adults. I do not use baby exercises. I use texts, letters, phone calls, real recordings, and I coach the student through them.

A typical lesson with Sofia

Eighty five minutes, structured for adults

01

Learning goals

  • One grammar point named at the start of the lesson
  • Ten to fifteen new items of Sicily-anchored vocabulary
  • One conversational function students will leave able to perform
  • One short cultural note tied to the day's material
  • A named piece of work to bring back next week
02

The eighty five minute flow

  • 0 to 10 · warm up in Italian, weekend and week ahead
  • 10 to 25 · new material, introduced through a real scene
  • 25 to 45 · guided practice, in pairs on breakout
  • 45 to 55 · a short break, Sofia stays online
  • 55 to 75 · free conversation on the day's theme
  • 75 to 85 · review, homework, questions
03

Between lesson work

  • One short reading from Sofia's Italian sources
  • One audio, usually a real recording under three minutes
  • One written piece, no longer than 120 words
  • A quiet fifteen minutes with Biagio, if you want it
  • Total realistic weekly load: ninety to a hundred minutes
