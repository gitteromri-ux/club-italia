The region

Campania, as Luca teaches it

Luca is from the Vomero hill in Naples. He read Italian philology at Federico II with a dissertation on twentieth century Neapolitan writing, then trained as a language teacher in Perugia. He teaches the highest level in our course ladder. His students are often preparing for an official CEFR B1 examination in the twelve months after his course.

Every teacher on our faculty teaches from one Italian region and from one only. That is not a marketing point. It is what allows a class to have a place. When Luca opens a lesson at seven in the morning her time, the light behind her is Napoli light. The bread on her desk was bought at a Napoli bakery. The vocabulary that emerges in a conversation about weekends is Campania vocabulary. Students on the other side of the world tell us the same thing, class after class. They are not on a language platform. They are in Napoli, for eighty five minutes.

Regional Italian is not dialect. Luca teaches the standard national language, the Italian of newspapers and universities and the state broadcaster. What she brings is location. A verb form is illustrated with a scene from her city. A grammar point is drawn out of a conversation that could have taken place on her street. The language stays standard. The teaching room is specific.

Teaching manifesto
I teach the last stage. My students arrive able to hold a conversation and leave with a language that can carry a professional email, a difficult phone call, a family visit that lasts a week. That transition is the hardest part of the whole course and it is the part I most enjoy. Naples is a demanding city and it made me a demanding teacher. I mark work carefully. I return it fast. I expect a student to work between our lessons and I say so on day one. Nothing about the last stage is passive. Neither am I.
Luca De Simone · Napoli

A typical lesson with Luca

Eighty five minutes, structured for adults

01

Learning goals

  • One grammar point named at the start of the lesson
  • Ten to fifteen new items of Campania-anchored vocabulary
  • One conversational function students will leave able to perform
  • One short cultural note tied to the day's material
  • A named piece of work to bring back next week
02

The eighty five minute flow

  • 0 to 10 · warm up in Italian, weekend and week ahead
  • 10 to 25 · new material, introduced through a real scene
  • 25 to 45 · guided practice, in pairs on breakout
  • 45 to 55 · a short break, Luca stays online
  • 55 to 75 · free conversation on the day's theme
  • 75 to 85 · review, homework, questions
03

Between lesson work

  • One short reading from Luca's Italian sources
  • One audio, usually a real recording under three minutes
  • One written piece, no longer than 120 words
  • A quiet fifteen minutes with Biagio, if you want it
  • Total realistic weekly load: ninety to a hundred minutes
