Inside a single lesson

Eighty-five minutes with Francesca.

Lesson 07 · In Airbnb. Every learner logs in at 8pm EST on a Tuesday. The camera opens on Francesca's desk in Venezia. This is what the next eighty-five minutes look like.

Learning goals

  • You can hold the scene of the lesson in Italian for four full turns.
  • You can use the new grammar without translating in your head.
  • You can pronounce the new vocabulary the way Francesca does.
  • You leave with two written examples in your own notebook.

The 85-minute flow

  1. 00:00 to 00:10 · Warm greetings and last week's carryover.
  2. 00:10 to 00:30 · The new scene, first read together, then acted out in pairs.
  3. 00:30 to 00:50 · Grammar in the moment. Francesca draws on the whiteboard.
  4. 00:50 to 01:10 · Pair rooms with focused correction from Francesca.
  5. 01:10 to 01:25 · Group reunion, final rehearsal, homework for next week.

Between-lesson work

  • A twelve-minute Biagio conversation on the new scene.
  • One short reading, one short listening, one short writing.
  • Optional: a matching cultural clip from our library.
  • Expected weekly load: about ninety minutes outside class.

The city is the classroom

Venice, the city that invented modern travel.

For a thousand years Venice was where east met west, and every ship that arrived at the Rialto brought a new set of loanwords into Italian. Every travel word you will learn this term has a Venetian ancestor.

Francesca teaches from a studio in Cannaregio, and if the class is at four in the afternoon, you will hear the water outside the window. She has taught travellers of every nationality for ten years and knows exactly which Italian sentences survive a real conversation with a train conductor.

By the end you will handle every transport, hotel, museum and mountain refuge you might meet on a real Italian trip, without switching to English once.
